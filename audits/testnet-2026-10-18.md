## 4. Load / abuse test (`node scripts/testnet-load.mjs` → `audits/testnet-load.json`)

40 throwaway wallets (36 fans + sponsor, venue, door scanner, artist), 1.2 MON each. **534 txs sent: 503 succeeded as intended, 31 were should-revert txs sent for real (all reverted on-chain), 0 unexpected reverts.** 171,399,414 gas, 17.4827 MON in fees.

- Check-ins: **720 attested visits** in 180 batched claims (36 fans × 5 past days × 4 stops), plus daily-cap and budget-exhaustion edges.
- Tickets: 108 sold for show A in 108 buy txs + 36 for show B + 5 for show C; 10 resales at exactly 110 % (artist royalty 2.75 rtUSDC); 36 door scans submitted by the fans themselves.
- Show A settled: gross 540 → artist 362.88, venue 155.52 (30 %), fee 21.6 (4 %).
- Show B venue-cancelled: deposit 50 back to the tour fund, 36/36 fans refunded exactly face value via 4 `refundAll(10)` calls.
- Show C artist-cancelled before showtime (leash allowance restored by B's cancel): 30 back to the fund, venue kept its 20 cancel fee; 5 tickets refunded.

## 6. Findings

- **No contract failures.** Every should-revert case reverted with the intended error, on simulation and on-chain; no unexpected success.
- Deploy wrapper bug fixed: newer forge prints the estimate as `… MON` instead of `… ETH`, so `npm run deploy:testnet` refused to broadcast ("simulation failed"). The regex now accepts both.
- The QA stack keeps the faucet token (rtUSDC) as its asset; its campaigns/venues/vaults are labelled `[QA load]` and live only on the QA addresses, never in the production deployments file.
- Throughput numbers are bounded by the public RPC rate limit; a measurement of chain capacity needs a private/paid RPC endpoint.
